# Rankbound pSEO research

Research date: **2026-11-10**. Market: United States.

## Data quality

- **Measured**: search volume, keyword difficulty (KD), traffic potential (TP), SERP composition and Domain Rating (DR), from Ahrefs (Keywords Explorer, SERP overview, free DR checker) on the research date. Ahrefs volumes are modeled monthly estimates, not exact counts.
- **Observed**: competitor pages read on the research date (URLs below). `fitbod.me/workouts` returned HTTP 403, so it is covered by SERP data only.
- **Not available**: Google Search Console. No GSC project is connected, so there are **no real query or impression numbers** for elevixapps.com yet. Google autocomplete was not queried.
- **Assumptions** are marked as such. Nothing here is a traffic or ranking forecast. No such forecast is made.

elevixapps.com has a Domain Rating of **24** (Ahrefs, 2026-11-10).

## Executive recommendation

Build a small set of **seven indexable pages** that share one rule-based workout engine, and reject the rest of the idea list.

1. The best opportunities are **tool-shaped searches where the current results are articles**: push-up volume ("how many push ups a day" 5,300/mo, KD 11; "100 push ups a day" 2,500, KD 3) and the One Punch Man routine ("one punch man workout" 4,800, KD 29; "saitama workout" 3,000, KD 23). No calculator ranks for either.
2. The core generator terms are real but modest ("workout generator" 600, "workout planner" 3,200, "ai workout generator" 1,000). The results are interactive tools, and sites with DR 3 to 8 rank, so a new page can compete.
3. The RPG and gamified terms ("fitness rpg" 80, "gamified workout app" 90, "rpg workout app" 50) have the highest download intent but tiny volume. They are served by the existing landing page, not by new pSEO pages.
4. The One Punch Man page is the one franchise page. Per the project's copy rules, franchise names appear only in a separate editorial page, never in branding or on the landing page. Solo Leveling is kept out.

### Gaps these findings point to

1. Generators gate results behind forms or accounts. A result that appears immediately, with no signup, is uncommon.
2. Gamified apps have no web tool at all. Their sites only push installs.
3. Anime content is static lists, plus membership upsells.
4. No push-up or One Punch Man calculator exists in the top results.
5. Few tools are built mobile-first around a single readable workout card.

## Google guidance that shaped the plan

- Helpful, people-first content: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- Spam policies (scaled content abuse, doorways): https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

Third-party commentary (2026) describes stronger enforcement against scaled low-value programmatic pages. It is anecdotal and unverified, but it supports a **small page count with genuinely different content and a working tool on each page**. Implications:

- Seven pages, not hundreds. No parameter or modifier pages.
- Query-string results (shared workout links) always canonicalize to the clean page URL and are not in the sitemap.
- Each page has different copy, a different sample result, its own FAQ, and a different default tool setting. Where a page's tool is the same engine (generator, no-equipment, 15-minute, anime), the pages differ by intent, locked controls, and written content, and are linked as related tools rather than listed as variants.
- FAQ structured data matches the visible text. Google restricts FAQ rich results for most sites, so it is included for consistency, with no expectation of a rich result.
- Pages are statically rendered, so content does not depend on JavaScript rendering.

Uncertainty to keep in mind:

- Ahrefs volumes are modeled and can be off by a wide margin for small keywords.
- AI Overviews appear on nearly every SERP in the keyword set, which usually lowers click-through.
- Video-heavy SERPs (time-based workouts, One Punch Man) may take clicks from a new tool page.

## SEO risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Pages judged near-duplicate (four generator-based pages) | Different intent, copy, FAQ, controls and sample results per page. Only seven URLs, each linked as a distinct tool |
| Scaled-content enforcement | No bulk generation, no parameter pages, a working tool and written guidance on every page |
| New site section with little authority (DR 24) | Target low-KD terms. Link from the landing page. Treat results as slow, and measure before expanding |
| AI Overviews and video SERPs reduce clicks | Offer something an overview cannot: an interactive result |
| Franchise and IP exposure on the OPM page | Nominative use only, no imagery, explicit no-affiliation notice, kept off the landing page and other tool pages. Solo Leveling is excluded |
| Health claims | Rule-based and general, with a stop-if-pain line, no outcome claims, conservative caps, and a medical-advice disclaimer on every page |
| Impression of web-to-app import | Every CTA says the app does not import the workout |
| "AI" mislabeling | The tool does not claim AI. Page copy says so |

## App-download conversion strategy

- The result is never gated. A visitor gets a full workout without signup, popups or an app download.
- Three CTAs per page: a small "Download Free" button in the header, a contextual panel after the result ("Turn this into a daily quest"), and a final panel. They share one button style and the copy "Download Free on the App Store".
- The copy is honest: "It does not import this workout, so build a similar daily quest." Rankbound's real mechanics are used: +100 XP per daily quest, once per day; F to S ranks; rest days that do not break a streak.
- Tracking: `ct=rankbound-<page>-<placement>` on every App Store link, plus a `data-cta` attribute picked up by the existing analytics. App Store Connect shows the `ct` values.
- CTA clicks do **not** equal installs. Use App Store Connect (impressions, product page views, first-time downloads by campaign) for downstream measurement.
